# Data coverage and release boundaries

Repository audit: 28 November 2026. Counts below were read directly from the local SQLite corpus, JSON manifests, and source notes; they describe indexed records, not scholarly completeness.

## Quran text, words, and morphology

- `lib/db/qurabic.db` contains 6,236 verse rows and 77,429 word rows.
- `words.arabic` in that database is Buckwalter transliteration, not Unicode Arabic. The random-verse endpoint now converts that field for fallback display and prefers Quran.com `text_uthmani` tokens by word location; it selects only `id`, `word_num`, and `arabic`, never the local word-translation column.
- The word table has 1,642 distinct non-empty Buckwalter root codes.
- `public/data/occurrences/` has a valid, non-empty JSON occurrence list for every one of the 1,642 root IDs (44,431 rows total). It also contains an extra `kh-w-f.json`; its 112 verse coordinates match `x-w-f.json`, but the records are not byte-identical, so the extra file is not treated as another root.
- Root detail pages use each root’s full local QAC lemma distribution; the largest root currently has 22 lemmas. The family diagram paginates six at a time so the full set remains browsable on narrow screens.
- Word-study family cards now retain every normalized QAC form/lemma group instead of truncating at 12. A corpus-wide pass found 1,642 roots; the largest family has 41 groups, and q-w-m has 38. The interface paginates 12 cards at a time and fetches contextual glosses for the active page only. Requests are capped at 48 locations, above the current observed page size.
- `lib/data/wbw/` still contains 114 legacy Quran.com surah exports (11,834,582 bytes total); application routes no longer read them. They remain tracked as requested, and Next.js output tracing excludes them from the deployment bundle. Their redistribution terms remain unresolved; retaining them in a public source repository still requires permission or another documented rights basis.
- The tracked WBW JSON set is 117 files (15,721,967 bytes): those 114 surah exports plus three aggregate gloss files (`wbw-derivative-glosses.json`, `wbw-english-glosses.json`, and `wbw-indonesian-glosses.json`).
- `lib/lexicon/data/qabas-lemma-ids.json` maps 77,429 word positions across 6,236 verses to Qabas lemma IDs. An ID/link is not a local dictionary definition.
- Root, lemma, and grammatical tags come from Quranic Arabic Corpus v0.4. Its [download terms](https://corpus.quran.com/download/) permit app use with attribution, while its [FAQ](https://corpus.quran.com/faq.jsp) separately describes downloaded annotation as non-commercial research data. Commercial app distribution remains unresolved; get written clarification before release.

## QUL resource review

- [Quranic Universal Library](https://qul.tarteel.ai/resources) currently lists 77,432 morphology records (roots, lemmas and stems), 220 translations (including 22 word-by-word sets), 115 tafsir resources and 3,158 ayah-topic records. Its credits say most resources were made or curated by community contributors.
- QUL's [integration guide](https://qul.tarteel.ai/docs/getting-started) recommends JSON for lighter integration and SQLite for larger query workloads; it warns that schemas differ by resource. Its [FAQ](https://qul.tarteel.ai/docs/faq) says commercial use must be checked against each dataset's own terms. Bulk download availability is not, by itself, reuse permission. Before adopting a QUL resource, record the exact dataset, author/source, licence, attribution, and whether redistribution inside Qurabic is allowed.
- QUL morphology provides word/ayah root, lemma and stem mappings; it does not provide classical root definitions. It is a useful cross-check for morphology, not a replacement root lexicon.
- [MAQAM Quranic Corpus](https://github.com/CAMeL-Lab/MAQAM-Quranic-Corpus), paper published at [ArabicNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.arabicnlp-main.11/), is integrated as a local English stem-gloss fallback. Its 77,429 coordinate rows contain lemma, English stem gloss, POS and root fields, including particles. Direct database audit found 77,429 unique coordinates and no empty glosses; 13,946 entries use particle/preposition POS labels. The paper describes manually annotated lexical fields and two native Arabic expert annotators; broader morphosyntax was manually validated on two surahs and extended computationally elsewhere. The dataset is CC BY 4.0 (separate from the repository code license). Qurabic retains only coordinate, lemma, gloss, POS and root; it does not copy MAQAM's Quran text or other morphosyntactic fields. The UI labels these glosses as English, including in Indonesian UI, and says attached prefixes or pronouns may not be included; they are not a verse translation, tafsir, or complete dictionary definition. Root values stay separate from QAC assignments. See the build script and third-party notice for pinned source, attribution and change details.
- The [Word root](https://qul.tarteel.ai/resources/morphology/quran-word-roots/licence) and [Ayah Topics](https://qul.tarteel.ai/resources/ayah-topics/45/licence) licence pages both say copyright information is unavailable. Do not bundle their downloads until rights are clarified. The topic page attributes ontology sources to Quranic Arabic Corpus and thematic topics to The Clear Quran.

## Lexical material

Arramooz full database: 30,213 noun rows and 10,637 verb rows across 5,573 non-empty Arabic roots. Arabic Stop Words 0.9 adds 24,841 source form records, including 1,924 rows tagged by the source as particles (`حرف`) across 72 source lemmas. Kamus pagination exposes Arramooz root matches in pages of eight, Tāj al-ʿArūs lookup matches in pages of 12, and Arabic Stop Words forms in pages of 60; loaded source rows remain visible if a later page fails. These are classifications and affix analyses, not lexical definitions; see `lib/lexicon/SOURCES.md`. The /kamus page searches Arabic forms and roots and displays original Arabic entries; it does not invent English translations. The source version is pinned in lib/data/ARRAMOOZ-ATTRIBUTION.md under GPL-2.

Arabic Roots and Derivatives by Ahmed Khorsi adds a separate Tāj al-ʿArūs form index: 142,786 records across 11,060 source roots. It contributes vocalized and unvocalized forms and stems, but no definitions or word classes. Of 1,642 Qurabic Quran-root entries, 1,539 match a source root exactly and 15 match one uniquely after spelling normalization; 3 normalized spellings are ambiguous and 85 have no match. Ambiguous roots are hidden instead of merged. The upstream project is marked Beta, its dump was last updated in 2013, and its associated method paper describes unsupervised extraction with accuracy limits. This improves access to non-Quran forms but does not make root-family coverage complete. See `lib/lexicon/SOURCES.md` and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

- The Lane manifest has 243,376 general lookup keys and 26,167 Quran-index keys. Exact Buckwalter root-key matching against the Quran index finds 1,290 of 1,642 roots; 352 do not have an exact key.
- A separate call-level coverage pass on 28 November 2026 used the same `getLaneRootRecord(rootBw, true)` resolver as root pages across all 1,642 local roots. Its Buckwalter variants found a Lane entry with non-empty sense text for 1,591 roots; 51 returned no Lane entry. An Arabic-index lookup fix then resolved five additional roots against exact normalized Arabic root spellings and verified the cited Lane entry IDs: `r-A-y` (Lane 3, p. 999), `j-h-l` (2, p. 477), `m-l-A` (7, p. 2729), `f-A-y` (6, p. 2326), and `m-A-y` (8, p. 3016). Current coverage is 1,596 roots with sense text and 46 without. This is index-resolution coverage, not proof that the selected Lane sense is the right meaning for every Quranic form, or that the automated match has been independently checked.
- The remaining 46 roots do not resolve through the same candidates in Lane's full manifest either. Qurabic must not invent or misattribute a Lane definition for them.
- A separate read-only check of the 5,191 root headings in the CC BY-SA Lane XML mirror listed by [arabic-lexicon](https://github.com/eliyahu-freedman/arabic-lexicon) matched three of the 46 remaining spellings (`k-h-f`, `k-b-k-b`, `v-y-b`). Their XML bodies are only cross-references (“See Supplement” or “See art.”), with no definition in the distributed files. They do not improve sourced definition coverage; Qurabic did not import them. See the mirror's [README](https://github.com/eliyahu-freedman/arabic-lexicon) and [data notice](https://github.com/eliyahu-freedman/arabic-lexicon/blob/main/NOTICE) for the source and ShareAlike terms.
- A Lane root match means the chunk contains one or more dictionary records linked to that root. It does not establish that every record is a core definition or that the automatic root match is correct. Lane is English; the indexed transcription and root matching have not all received independent scholarly review. Keep the printed part and page beside each excerpt.
- Research lead checked 28 November 2026: [arabic-lexicon](https://github.com/eliyahu-freedman/arabic-lexicon) reports root-indexed full articles for Lane (~5,300 roots) and Lisān al-ʿArab (~8,450 roots). Its `NOTICE` says Lane’s digital text is CC BY-SA, but explicitly leaves the exact Lisān digital-edition source and license unconfirmed. Do not import or distribute its Lisān text until that is resolved; the repository’s count is not an independently verified coverage result.
- OpenITI's current [terms](https://openiti.org/docs/Terms%20of%20Use.html) describe most corpus material as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and its [copyright guidance](https://openiti.org/docs/Copyright_Questions.html) states its public-domain position for pre-1900 source texts. The corpus also warns that many digital texts originate from third-party collections and may contain transcription errors. This is a promising research corpus for locating a classical text such as Lisān al-ʿArab, but the non-commercial dataset terms and text-specific provenance do not establish permission for Play distribution; do not bulk-import it without a compatible written grant and verification of the exact text/edition.
- Research lead checked 28 November 2026: [alelm-lab/resources](https://github.com/alelm-lab/resources) publishes research lexicons for roots, patterns, particles, and related Arabic morphology, but its public repository tree has no license file or reuse terms. It may be useful for comparison after permission and provenance are established; it is not currently bundled or presented as Qurabic dictionary meaning data.
- Direct count of the local `quran-bil-quran-roots.json` export: 1,651 distinct Arabic root records, each with an English summary; 1,157 summaries match a local QAC root and include an al-Mufradat Arabic excerpt after whitespace/alif normalization. Five local roots do not match this export (`n-w-s`, `n-d-w`, `T-m-n`, `l-A-l-A`, `m-E-n`); the application has separate QAC-specific gloss profiles for those IDs. The count describes field presence and normalized root matching, not scholarly review of every summary.
- A separate scan found 1,163 non-empty `mufradatArabic` fields in the export; 561 visibly end with an ellipsis. The export provides no root-level volume/page citations. The app now labels these as excerpts that may be truncated and links to the source project; they are not presented as complete dictionary entries.
- The upstream README says its thematic-analysis pipeline uses no LLM, but does not say whether the English root summaries were written or reviewed with AI. The app now labels that authorship and review process as undocumented; the README's pipeline claim is not evidence that the root summaries are AI-free.
- The upstream Quran bil-Quran repository declares MIT, but its README also credits third-party works. That repository-level license does not by itself establish redistribution rights for every bundled source or excerpt; resolve those rights before including the summaries in a public app release.
- Combined root meaning coverage from the local source-resolution pass: 1,596 roots have a Lane entry with sense text, 1,637 have a Quran bil-Quran English summary, and 1,640 have at least one of those. The remaining two (`n-d-w`, `l-A-l-A`) have separate QAC-specific gloss profiles. This is not complete classical-dictionary coverage: most Quran bil-Quran summaries lack root-level page citations and their authoring/review process is undocumented; 46 roots still lack a Lane entry through Qurabic's resolver. Keep the source and review limits visible in the UI and release claims.
- Qabas links lead to lemma records hosted by Birzeit University; Qurabic does not bundle their definitions.
- QAC/QUL morphology can identify forms and grammatical roles. It does not, on its own, supply a classical root meaning.
- Root entries without an indexed Lane definition automatically open their matching Arramooz form list when available. Arramooz forms remain in original Arabic and load 60 at a time; a visible count and next-page control keep larger roots navigable without loading every record into memory at once. This exposes sourced material without inventing an Indonesian or English translation.

## Translation provenance

- Indonesian and English word-by-word glosses are requested at runtime from Quran.com API v4 by verse and word coordinate. The API response does not give a translator for every word; do not label these glosses as Kemenag.
- When Quran.com has no gloss for a requested word or cannot be reached, the local MAQAM CC BY 4.0 lexical-gloss index fills available coordinates with a visibly attributed English gloss. This fallback is never labeled as Indonesian translation; Quran.com remains preferred when it supplies a coordinate gloss.
- A read-only coordinate audit matched all 77,429 positions in the 114 WBW files to non-empty Indonesian and English glosses (77,429 each, with no missing or extra positions). This verifies coverage, not the accuracy or translator identity of each gloss. Both exports are from Quran.com API v4.
- That audit describes the retired local exports, not a guarantee of live API availability. Runtime calls are limited to the selected verse coordinates; word glosses are currently online-only and have not yet been rechecked by Playwright in this implementation pass.
- Application routes for root entries, word study, random ayah, and on-demand word meanings in search results no longer import or read the bundled Quran.com gloss exports; they request only active word locations at runtime. Multi-verse requests retain successful glosses when another verse fetch fails; a total upstream failure remains an error. The old exports remain tracked in the repository and are excluded from deployment output. Quran Foundation's current Developer Terms prohibit publishing raw API content as a dataset or app package and require Content Sync for offline copies when a matching resource is available. See [Developer Terms](https://api-docs.quran.com/legal/developer-terms/).
- Whole-verse English reading uses Saheeh International (resource 20); Indonesian whole-verse reading uses Kemenag RI (resource 33). Keep those sources distinct from word glosses.
- English verse-search matches come from Quran Foundation Search API; canonical Arabic is read from the local Qurabic database and Saheeh International resource 20 from Quran Foundation Content API. Production search scope and response coverage are not yet verified.

## Audio terjemahan manusia

- Audio English per ayat dari Rowwad tersedia di QuranEnc. Syarat QuranEnc yang dipublikasikan membahas penerbitan ulang **teks terjemahan**, bukan distribusi berkas audio, sehingga belum menjadi izin audio untuk Qurabic. Tetap nonaktif sampai pemegang hak rekaman memberi izin yang jelas; Quran Foundation juga meminta hak rekaman diperiksa per edisi dan menyarankan menghubungi pemegang hak. [Syarat terjemahan Rowwad](https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad) · [FAQ Quran Foundation](https://api-docs.quran.com/docs/tutorials/faq/).
- ClearQuran offers verse-by-verse English translation audio and its publisher explicitly permits commercial reuse, conversion, splitting, and combining audio with attribution while keeping the translation wording unchanged. The page labels voice variants as male, female, and classic, but does not identify the narrator or say whether these files are human recordings or synthetic speech. Rights look promising; the human-only requirement is still unverified, so do not enable the audio until ClearQuran confirms the recording method and edition. [ClearQuran audio and terms](https://clearquran.com/download/).
- Sebuah dataset publik audio terjemahan Indonesia per ayat menyebut 6.236 rekaman dibuat dengan Gemini TTS. Karena bukan rekaman manusia dan melanggar kebutuhan suara manusia yang ditetapkan untuk Qurabic, dataset ini ditolak meskipun menyediakan berkas massal. [Metadata dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/insanalamin/quran-terjemahan-indonesia-audio-by-ayah).
- Handsfree terjemahan manusia Indonesia maupun English tetap dinonaktifkan pada build publik sampai rekaman, hak distribusi, serta cakupan 6.236 ayat diverifikasi. Tilawah manusia berbahasa Arab tetap menjadi alur audio tersendiri.

## Tafsir availability and provenance

- A read-only audit of pinned revision [`73b1d52`](https://github.com/rioastamal/quran-json/tree/73b1d5262f30695f5d17d9a61ce6b6af9b74aeda) on 28 November 2026 fetched all 114 `surah/{n}.json` files. Each had non-empty text in `tafsir.id.kemenag.text`; the total was 6,236 entries. The per-surah entry counts matched Qurabic's canonical ayah counts.
- This verifies field coverage in that public archive, not the authenticity, editorial completeness, or primary-source provenance of every tafsir passage. The repository README attributes Quran text and translations to Kemenag but does not separately document tafsir provenance. GitHub reports an MIT repository license; rights for the underlying tafsir text still need confirmation before bundling or claiming official provenance.
- Qurabic fetches tafsir from the archive at runtime and identifies the archive and its dataset label in the source line. Missing text is not replaced with generated commentary. LPMQ's official API separately offers Quran text, 2019 translations, Tafsir Ringkas, and Tafsir Tahlili after registration, a request letter, and a survey; Qurabic has no access credentials for it yet: [official API access procedure](https://quran-api.lpmqkemenag.id/).

## Release blockers from this audit

- Do not claim complete root definitions: 46 of 1,642 Quran roots return no Lane entry from the current resolver, Lane text remains English, and the other summary dataset's authoring/review process is undocumented.
- Runtime app imports were replaced with coordinate-based Quran.com API requests. The repository still tracks the 114 legacy exports (11,834,582 bytes); Next.js excludes them from deployment output, but their continued presence in a public repository remains a rights question that must be resolved before release.
- Quranic particle entries use concise bilingual Qurabic notes, visibly labeled as AI-assisted and not scholar-reviewed. Where a known particle has no hand-authored note, the entry derives a cautious explanation from its recorded particle category; unknown particles do not receive a generated placeholder explanation. These notes are learning aids, not verified lexicon definitions.
- `lib/data/roots-summary.json` contains Indonesian root summaries but has no per-entry source metadata. Legacy canonical resolver helpers can still place those summaries into semantic fields. WordStudy prefers live coordinate-matched glosses; if those fail, it may show a non-generic Indonesian legacy gloss with an explicit unrecorded-provenance badge. This is a disclosed fallback, not a verified definition. Generic morphology labels remain excluded from the meaning slot.
- `lib/data/root-semantics.ts` also retains selected legacy explanatory profiles whose source and review status vary by entry. Their module documentation now says to use explicit source metadata before presenting them as definitions. Audit their rendered call sites before release; do not infer a dictionary meaning from QAC root membership.
- Legacy word glosses no longer claim QAC/Kemenag verification. Where one is used as a fallback, the word entry labels its source and review history as unrecorded and links to an explanatory source note. Runtime Quran.com glosses and cited Lane excerpts retain separate attribution.
- Qabas lemma coverage is positional coverage only; it is not proof that every linked page is available, licensed for republishing, or a complete lexical explanation.
- Birzeit's current Qabas overview counts 473 functional-word entries and describes the database as a graph linking lemmas to source lexicons, rather than a bulk export of their protected definitions. Its published research says most source dictionaries are not open to developers. Qabas is useful for lemma discovery and external lookup, but it does not close Qurabic's need for licensed, readable particle/root definitions. See [Qabas overview](https://sina.birzeit.edu/qabas/about) and the [Qabas research paper](https://jarrar.info/publications/JH24.pdf).
- Quran Foundation Content Sync stores Quran text and verse translations, not root definitions, particle classifications, or tafsir. Separately, the service worker caches MAQAM's English stem-gloss index with the reader shell for offline per-word fallback; it does not provide Indonesian offline word translations. See [Android offline behavior](android-offline.md).
- A possible supplementary root-analysis source is al-quran.fr's *Root Dictionary*, Edition 1.6. Its PDF explicitly says the translation and dictionary are CC BY 4.0 and that AI assisted its author, AbulIlyass, who checks and makes the final decision. The currently listed English PDF is Volume 1 and covers roots from ا through ض; it cannot alone fill the complete root index, and its method is a Quran-text-based synthesis rather than an independently reviewed classical dictionary. Do not merge it into the sourced Lane definition slot. Reassess as a clearly labeled, supplementary study note only after verifying the remaining volume(s), root coverage, citations, and its source site's reuse terms. Source: [al-quran.fr Root Dictionary, Edition 1.6](https://www.al-quran.fr/downloads/dictionnaire_racines_en_v1.6_papier_tome1.pdf).
- Quran Foundation's [FAQ](https://api-docs.quran.com/docs/tutorials/faq/) and [Content Sync guide](https://api-docs.quran.com/docs/tutorials/content-sync/getting-started/) confirm that offline sync supports eligible `word_by_word_translations` resources and is the required route when a resource is available there. The official snapshot page's `85` row is an API schema example; it does not prove that resource `85` is currently published, approved for this app, or licensed for a particular use. This workspace has no verified production resource IDs, client approval, or complete snapshots for English and Indonesian word meanings. Do not enable or claim bilingual offline word meanings until those are confirmed from the production catalog and both snapshots pass coverage and attribution checks.
- QUL lists bulk Indonesian word-by-word translation resource `96`, with location-keyed JSON/SQLite data. Its own licence page reports that copyright information is unavailable, so the dataset is a research lead and cannot be bundled or served by Qurabic until rights are clarified: [resource and format](https://qul.tarteel.ai/resources/translation/96/docs), [licence status](https://qul.tarteel.ai/resources/translation/96/licence).
